\section{Allocation identity}

Let agent $i$ use a positive differentiable influence kernel
$f_\kappa(x_i,b)$ and let
\[
G_i(\bx,b)=\frac{f_\kappa(x_i,b)}{\sum_j f_\kappa(x_j,b)},
\qquad
u_i(\bx;B)=\E_{b\sim B}[G_i(\bx,b)].
\]
Writing $s_i(b)=\nabla_{x_i}\log f_\kappa(x_i,b)$ gives the exact identity
\[
\nabla_{x_i}u_i
=\E_{b\sim B}\!\left[G_i(b)(1-G_i(b))s_i(b)\right].
\]
The score must belong to the configured kernel.  Replacing it with $b-x_i$
is valid only for an isotropic Gaussian, up to a constant metric factor.

\section{Offline theory versus online observations}

The non-naive theory initialization and the $\sigma^*$ calculation use the
offline KDE quadrature
\[
B_{\rm KDE}=\{(b_k,B_k)\}_{k=1}^{K},\qquad \sum_k B_k=1,
\]
stored as \cfg{TraitSpace.grid} and \cfg{TraitSpace.weights}.  Their gradient
is
\[
g_i^{\rm off}=\sum_k B_kG_i(b_k)(1-G_i(b_k))s_i(b_k).
\]

The trained router is online: it does not know the complete resource
distribution.  In round $t$ it observes only the training batch
$\mathcal B_t=\{b_{tm}\}_{m=1}^{M_t}$ and uses
\[
\widehat g_i^{(t)}=
\frac{1}{M_t}\sum_{m=1}^{M_t}
G_i(b_{tm})(1-G_i(b_{tm}))s_i(b_{tm}).
\]
Thus the online rule is the exact gradient of the empirical batch game and a
stochastic approximation to the training-resource law.  It is intentionally
not equal to $g_i^{\rm off}$ on every round.  Validation, test, and KDE-grid
resources are not used by the normal online update.  An offline-KDE update is
retained only as an oracle diagnostic.

Soft top-$k$ controls which adapters train on each observed prompt.  It does
not truncate the sum defining $\widehat g_i^{(t)}$: all observed prompts and
all clone-level allocations enter the position gradient.

\section{Constrained simultaneous step}

Let $P_D$ be the tangent projector and $\Pi_D$ the Euclidean projection for
the position domain $D$.  The box uses the identity tangent projector.  For
the probability simplex $\Delta^{L-1}$,
\[
P_\Delta=I-\frac1L\one\one^\top.
\]
One scalar is applied to the complete population gradient:
\[
\eta_{\rm eff}=\min\left\{\eta,
\frac{c}{\max_i\|P_D\widehat g_i\|_2}\right\},
\qquad
x_i^+=\Pi_D\!\left(x_i+\eta_{\rm eff}P_D\widehat g_i\right).
\]
This cap preserves the joint vector-field direction.  There is no per-agent
division by $\sum_mG_i(1-G_i)$ and no inverse-Hessian preconditioner.

\section{Implemented kernel scores}

Let $d=b-x$, let $C\succ0$ be the configured shape, and let
$r=\sqrt{\delta^2+d^\top C^{-1}d}$.
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{p{0.18\linewidth}p{0.72\linewidth}}
\toprule
Kernel & Position score $s(x,b)$ \\
\midrule
Gaussian & $C^{-1}(b-x)/\sigma^2$ \\
Hyperbolic & $C^{-1}(b-x)/(\sigma r)$ \\
Dirichlet & $\sigma^{-1}[\log b-\psi(\alpha)+\psi(\alpha_0)\one]$,
where $\alpha=\one+x/\sigma$ \\
Product--Beta & $\sigma^{-1}[\log b-\log(1-b)-\psi(\alpha)+\psi(\beta)]$,
where $\alpha=\one+x/\sigma$ and $\beta=\one+(1-x)/\sigma$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

For the Dirichlet kernel $x\in\Delta^{L-1}$ and
\[
\alpha_0-L=\frac1\sigma,
\qquad
\operatorname{mode}\operatorname{Dir}(\alpha)
=\frac{\alpha-\one}{\alpha_0-L}=x.
\]
It is therefore mode-parameterized, not mean-parameterized.  The common
$\psi(\alpha_0)\one$ term is part of the full ambient derivative and vanishes
only after projection onto the simplex tangent.

\section{Kernel-matched stability}

At a symmetric equilibrium $x^*$ found from
$P_D\E_B[s(x^*,b)]=0$, the antisymmetric population mode is
\[
A_-=\frac{1}{N^2}\left[(N-1)\E_B[H]+(N-2)\E_B[ss^\top]\right],
\qquad H=\nabla_x^2\log f_\kappa.
\]
On the simplex the active matrix is $Q^\top A_-Q$, where the columns of $Q$
span $\one^\perp$.

For the hyperbolic family, $x^*$ does not depend on $\sigma$.  Evaluating at
$\sigma=1$, define the positive log-curvature and score scatter
\[
K=-\E_B[H_1(b)],\qquad C=\E_B[s_1(b)s_1(b)^\top].
\]
After restricting both matrices to the active domain (replace them by
$Q^\top KQ$ and $Q^\top CQ$ on the simplex), the supplied closed form is
\[
\sigma^*_{\rm hyp}=\frac{N-2}{N-1}\,
\lambda_{\max}\!\left(K^{-1/2}CK^{-1/2}\right).
\]
The implementation uses a Cholesky reduction of this symmetric generalized
eigenproblem.  Dirichlet and product--Beta thresholds, whose equilibria move
with $\sigma$, are instead found by a warm-started logarithmic scan followed
by Brent refinement of the first positive-to-negative largest-eigenvalue
crossing.

\section{Compatibility}

An explicit \cfg{kernel} block defaults to
\cfg{position\_update: game\_gradient}.  Configurations without that block
continue through the pre-existing Gaussian allocation, centroid update, and
analytic threshold implementation unchanged.  The historical
\cfg{theory\_matched} centroid is parallel to the isotropic Gaussian gradient
on the same resources, but its per-agent normalization gives different
simultaneous dynamics and it is not a general-kernel update.
