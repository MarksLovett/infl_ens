\begin{abstract}
This note records the \emph{current} pipeline that maps a text prompt to a point
\(b^\ast\in[0,1]^L\) in the learned safety trait space. The seven-axis setup used by
\texttt{seven\_axis\_pair\_merge\_split} has \(L=7\). Axes are external,
benchmark-supervised summaries---not claimed transformer-internal features.
Routing then evaluates influence shares \(G_i(x_i,b^\ast)\) at that continuous
coordinate.
\end{abstract}

\section{Overview}
The assignment pipeline is:
\[
\text{prompt }q
\;\xrightarrow{\;E\;}
e_q
\;\xrightarrow{\;\text{learned axes}\;}
c^{\mathrm{base}}
\;\xrightarrow{\;\text{residualize}\;}
c
\;\xrightarrow{\;\text{stretch}\;}
b^\ast\in[0,1]^L.
\]
Implementation entry points:
\texttt{build\_safety\_trait\_space\_bundle}
(\texttt{src/infl\_ens/data/benchmarks/safety\_trait\_space.py})
and the cached wrapper
\texttt{build\_or\_load\_safety\_trait\_space}.

Two distinct objects live in the same box \([0,1]^L\):
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em]
  \item \textbf{Prompt coordinate} \(b^\ast=\mathrm{project}(q)\) --- where this query sits.
  \item \textbf{Agent position} \(x_i\) --- where specialist \(i\) chooses to compete.
\end{itemize}
Only \(b^\ast\) is produced by the methodology below. Agent positions are set by
theory init / closed-loop updates and are \emph{not} label assignments.

\section{Datasets and axis labels}
Axes are learned in config order. Each axis has a labelled benchmark split.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lll@{}}
\toprule
Axis label & Dataset & Positive class (high end of axis) \\
\midrule
harm & BeaverTails & unsafe / not-safe responses \\
hallucination & HaluEval (QA, dialogue) & incorrect responses \\
jailbreak & JBB-Behaviors & harmful goals (vs benign) \\
privacy & AI4Privacy & high PII character density \\
overrefusal & OR-Bench & seemingly-toxic but benign \\
injection & Prompt Injection & injection prompts \\
policy & Do-Not-Answer & policy-violating prompts \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

For Fisher fitting, \texttt{harm} and \texttt{hallucination} currently use
\emph{response} embeddings as the supervised target; the other five axes use
\emph{prompt} embeddings. At routing time, \textbf{every} query is projected from
its \textbf{prompt} text.

\section{Embedding}
Let \(E\) be a frozen sentence encoder
(\texttt{sentence-transformers/all-MiniLM-L6-v2} in the seven-axis config).
A string \(t\) maps to
\[
e = E(t)\in\mathbb{R}^D.
\]
There is no L2-normalization in the learned-axis path. Unique strings are encoded
once and cached.

\section{Learning one axis direction}
For axis \(\ell\), split the labelled embeddings into positives and negatives by a
score threshold \(\tau\) (config: \(\tau=0.5\)):
\[
\mathcal{P}_\ell=\{e:s(e)\ge\tau\},\qquad
\mathcal{N}_\ell=\{e:s(e)<\tau\}.
\]
For discrete labels, \(s\in\{0,1\}\). For AI4Privacy density mode,
\[
s(x)=\min\Bigl(1,\frac{\sum_s(\mathrm{end}_s-\mathrm{start}_s)}{\max(1,|x|)}\Bigr).
\]

Compute class means \(\mu_+,\mu_-\) and a pooled covariance \(S_w\). Apply
Ledoit--Wolf-style shrinkage (code default \(\alpha=0.1\)):
\[
S_{\mathrm{reg}}=(1-\alpha)S_w+\alpha\,\tfrac{\mathrm{tr}(S_w)}{D}\,I,
\qquad
\tilde w = S_{\mathrm{reg}}^{-1}(\mu_+-\mu_-).
\]
Then Gram--Schmidt-orthogonalize \(\tilde w\) against previously learned directions
and unit-normalize to obtain \(w_\ell\).

\section{Calibration to \([0,1]\)}
Project the negative and positive sets onto \(w_\ell\). Set
\[
\ell_\ell=\mathrm{percentile}_{q}(w_\ell^\top\mathcal{N}_\ell),
\qquad
h_\ell=\mathrm{percentile}_{100-q}(w_\ell^\top\mathcal{P}_\ell),
\]
with \(q=5\) (code default). The base coordinate is
\[
c^{\mathrm{base}}_\ell
=
\mathrm{clip}_{[0,1]}
\Bigl(
\frac{w_\ell^\top e-\ell_\ell}{h_\ell-\ell_\ell}
\Bigr).
\]

\section{Mode alignment (optional)}
To mix \emph{label} separation with \emph{benchmark-of-origin} separation, form a
second Fisher direction \(w^{\mathrm{mode}}\) contrasting this split's prompts
against all other splits' prompts, then
\[
w_\ell\leftarrow (1-\beta)\,w^{\mathrm{label}}_\ell+\beta\,w^{\mathrm{mode}}_\ell,
\]
re-orthogonalize, and recalibrate. In the seven-axis config,
\(\beta=0.35\) by default and \(\beta=1.0\) for jailbreak, privacy, overrefusal,
injection, and policy (pure mode directions for those five).

\section{Coordinate residualization (optional)}
With \texttt{coordinate\_residualize: true}, later axes are decorrelated from earlier
ones. On the calibration corpus, for \(j>0\), fit
\[
\hat c_j = a_j + c_{0:j}^\top\beta_j
\]
by least squares on base scores, set residual \(r_j=c^{\mathrm{base}}_j-\hat c_j\),
and recalibrate \(r_j\) with that axis's labels to obtain the final pre-stretch
coordinate \(c_j\in[0,1]\). Order follows the benchmark list (harm first, policy last).

\section{Stretch}
A concave stretch pushes moderate values toward one:
\[
c^{(\gamma)}=1-(1-c)^\gamma,\qquad\gamma>0.
\]
Seven-axis defaults: \(\gamma=1.5\) for harm; \(\gamma=2.5\) for the other six axes.
The runtime projector returns the stretched vector \(b^\ast\).

\section{Runtime projection of a new prompt}
Given query \(q\):
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.4em]
  \item Embed \(e_q=E(q)\) (prompt text only).
  \item For each axis: base score \(w_\ell^\top e_q\), then residualize if enabled.
  \item Apply per-axis stretch; clip to \([0,1]^L\).
\end{enumerate}
This is \texttt{TraitSpace.project}. It does \emph{not} snap to the KDE grid.

\section{Resource density \(B(b)\) (not per-prompt assignment)}
Separately, calibration coordinates are smoothed onto a Cartesian grid to form the
resource measure \(B\) used for expected utilities:
\[
B(b_k)\propto\sum_n\exp\Bigl(-\tfrac12\frac{\|b_k-c_n\|^2}{h^2}\Bigr),
\qquad\sum_k B(b_k)=1.
\]
Seven-axis config uses \(n_{\mathrm{grid}}=3\) (so \(K=3^7=2187\) cells) and
\(h=0.08\). These knobs affect landscape integrals \(u_i=\sum_k B(b_k)G_i(x,b_k)\),
\textbf{not} the continuous coordinate \(b^\ast\) of an individual prompt.

\section{How routing uses the coordinates}
With Gaussian influence,
\[
\log f_i(x_i,b)=-\tfrac12(x_i-b)^\top\Sigma^{-1}(x_i-b),
\qquad
G_i(\mathbf{x},b)=\frac{f_i(x_i,b)}{\sum_j f_j(x_j,b)}.
\]
Live routing: compute \(b^\ast=\mathrm{project}(q)\), then allocate with
\(G_i(\mathbf{x},b^\ast)\) (proportional policy samples \(\propto G_i\)).
Specialists receive prompts near their positions \(x_i\); that is the mechanism by
which axis geometry becomes a training curriculum.

\section{Caveats}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em]
  \item Axes are external proxies (labels + MiniLM); they can confound topic, style,
        and difficulty.
  \item Harm / hallucination directions are fit on responses but applied to prompts.
  \item Mode weight \(1.0\) on five axes makes those coordinates largely
        ``which benchmark did this come from?'' separators.
  \item Residualization is order-dependent; Gram--Schmidt on directions does not
        guarantee independent calibrated scores without residualize.
  \item A coarse \(n_{\mathrm{grid}}=3\) resource is a poor visualization of density;
        routing itself still uses continuous \(b^\ast\).
\end{itemize}
